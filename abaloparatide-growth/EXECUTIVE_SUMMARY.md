# Executive summary — abaloparatide & final long-bone length

**Question.** In a growing organism with open **conventional** long-bone physes, does intermittent
systemic **abaloparatide** change **final mineralized long-bone length / adult height**?

**Answer (one line).** *Not identifiable from direct evidence; forced best estimate ≈ **approximately
neutral** for final length (mild, uncertain negative skew), at low confidence.*

**Forced probabilities** (reserve-limited growth-plate model, equal prior weights, mature plate):
P(any benefit)=**0.36** · P(meaningful benefit ≥1%/≥1 cm)=**0.13** · P(any harm)=**0.64**
(meaningful ≤−1%: **0.30**) · P(approximately neutral, |ΔL|<1%)=**0.57** · median ΔL≈**−0.3%** ·
95% interval **[−10.6%, +5.3%]**. Sign flips across priors → **model-dependent / not identified**.

## Why

1. **No direct evidence exists.** No study gives systemic abaloparatide to normal juveniles with
   conventional physes and measures long-bone length. The only growing-organism abaloparatide data are
   **mandibular condyle** (secondary cartilage) — a different tissue we do not transfer.
2. **The best analogue is short-term only.** Intermittent PTH(1-34) raised growth-plate thickness and
   growth *rate* in young rats (Ogawa 2002) — velocity, not final height, and gone in adults.
3. **Human exposure = neutral, and about replacement not augmentation.** Children on PTH(1-34)
   replacement grow normally (randomized vs calcitriol: no difference); this cannot show augmentation,
   and serial bone ages are unavailable.
4. **Genetics are an inverted-U.** Both loss (premature fusion) and constitutive gain (Jansen,
   disorganized) of PTH1R signaling **shorten** bones — "more signaling = more growth" is false.
5. **Velocity is usually not banked as height.** Aromatase inhibitors raise *predicted* height +5.9 cm
   but did **not** raise *attained* adult height — the decisive cautionary calibration.
6. **Mechanistic model:** growth is **reserve-limited** (a finite stem pool + a fusion clock). Even a
   growth-promoting assumption often turns final length **negative** because transient stimulation
   **spends the reserve faster → earlier fusion**. Only a **late-pubertal** window leans positive.

## Bottom line for a decision-maker
Do not expect a reliable adult-height gain from intermittent systemic abaloparatide on current
evidence; a **transient growth-velocity increase is plausible but likely spent on maturation tempo,
not final height.** The one experiment that would settle it: **final long-bone length + time-to-fusion
in normal juveniles dosed intermittently with washout to maturity.** (Safety — adult-rat osteosarcoma
signal — is reported separately and did not shape this efficacy estimate.)
